- **Circles → complex numbers → 2D rotation**: first showing how points on a unit circle correspond to complex numbers of unit magnitude, and how multiplying by such a number rotates the plane.
- **Spheres → quaternions → 3D/4D rotation**: extending this idea, showing how points on a sphere (and eventually a hypersphere) relate to rotation groups in higher dimensions.

**Key ideas usually covered**

1. **The unit sphere S²** as the natural generalization of the unit circle — points equidistant from the origin in 3D space, satisfying x² + y² + z² = 1.
2. **Great circles** as geodesics on the sphere, analogous to straight lines on a plane — important later for visualizing rotation paths.
3. **Stereographic projection**, a technique for mapping the sphere onto a plane, which Hanson uses extensively throughout the book to make 4D quaternion spaces comprehensible in 2D/3D images.
4. **The jump from S² to S³**: since unit quaternions live on the 3-sphere (a sphere in 4D space), the visualization of the ordinary sphere is meant to build intuition before tackling this harder-to-picture object.
5. **Symmetry and rotation groups**: how rotating a sphere rigidly maps it to itself, setting up the connection between sphere geometry and rotation groups (SO(3), and later SU(2) via quaternions).
