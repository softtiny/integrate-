
- **Polar form of complex numbers**: expressing a complex number as z = r(cos θ + i sin θ), where r is the magnitude and θ is the angle from the positive real axis, rather than the rectangular form a + bi.
- **Euler's formula connection**: z = re^(iθ), tying the polar form to the exponential representation, which is central to how complex numbers describe rotation.
- **Rotation interpretation**: multiplying two complex numbers in polar form adds their angles — this is the key geometric fact (rotation composition = angle addition) that makes polar form so relevant to a book about rotations.
- **De Moivre's theorem**: (cos θ + i sin θ)^n = cos(nθ) + i sin(nθ), often introduced here as a natural consequence of polar multiplication.
- **Setting up the 3D question**: since polar form makes 2D rotation so clean and compact, this section likely motivates the next step — asking whether a similar polar/exponential representation could describe rotations in 3D
